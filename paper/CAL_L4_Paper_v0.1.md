## Abstract

The Cognitive Abstraction Layer (CAL) architecture proposes that scalable AI governance requires not larger context windows, but semantically conservative hierarchical compression. Layer 4 (L4) — this paper — addresses the top of this hierarchy: meta-inference over compressed tensor volumes V without human working memory as a substrate.

We formalize the **L4 Efficiency Hypothesis**: there exists an inference architecture such that the cost of M(V) scales with κ(V) — the structural complexity of V — where κ(V) grows significantly more slowly than O(n²) in the number of raw artifacts n. This decouples inference cost from artifact volume and couples it to governance state complexity.

We introduce the **Representational Convergence Conjecture (RCC)**: that the representation optimal for human governance (CAL's V at high SID) and the representation optimal for the generator's own computation converge — or are related by a tractable translation. If true, a single compressed state would simultaneously serve human oversight and generator compute-efficiency: the *north star* of the architecture.

This paper reports the pre-experimental framework. Three conditions must be met before the L4 Efficiency Hypothesis can be empirically tested: (a) the composition operator C (L3) must be defined and validated, (b) the O(n²) flat-context baseline must be empirically measured on hardware, and (c) governance accuracy under both approaches must be quantified. Condition (b) is active: AMD-Instinct Labs measures the flat-context O(n²) curve on MI300X via `fa_dme`. Conditions (a) and (c) are deferred to their respective gates.

**Keywords:** meta-inference, efficiency hypothesis, semantic compression, governance inference, tensor volume, representational convergence, AMD-Instinct, flash attention

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 The Inference Cost Problem

Current transformer architectures pay an attention cost that scales with O(n²) in context length n. For AI governance over multi-agent pipelines, n is the number of raw artifacts — code files, logs, configs — generated by concurrent agents. As agent pipelines scale, this cost becomes a structural barrier: not a hardware constraint, but an architectural one. Flat-context inference over L0 artifacts re-evaluates every pairwise relationship at every forward pass, including relationships that are semantically redundant given the pipeline's causal structure.

The CAL hypothesis — instantiated at L2 by TCO-L2 [Chancay 2026] — is that semantic compression preserves the governance-relevant structure while eliminating this redundancy. L3 [Chancay 2026b] defines the composition operator C that produces the compressed volume V. L4 defines what M(V) can do with V — and at what cost.

### 1.2 Position in the CAL Architecture

```
L4  Meta-Inference         M(V) → {decisions, predictions, adaptations}    ← THIS PAPER
     ↑ gate-C: requires V from L3
L3  Tensor Volume          V = C({T⁽ˢ⁾}_{s=1}^{n})                         ← [Chancay 2026b]
     ↑ aggregates T from L2 sessions
L2  Cognitive Tensor       T[d,i,j,k] + NCF                                  ← TCO-L2 [Chancay 2026]
L1  Semantic Features      quality vectors V ∈ [0,1]¹¹
L0  Raw Artifacts          AI-generated code, configs, logs
```

L4 is the fully automated inference layer. No human working memory is required as the inference substrate — but this autonomy is conditional: M(V) is only coherent if C (L3) is semantically conservative. If C fails to preserve causal structure, M(V) degrades silently.

## 2. Formal Definition

```
M: V → {decisions, predictions, adaptations}
```

The meta-inference function M operates on the tensor volume V produced by the L3 composition operator C. Because V (by the properties required of C) encodes causal relationships, temporal coherence, and dimensional stability, M can perform reasoning that is structurally impossible at lower layers:

| Capability | Description | L2 analog |
|------------|-------------|-----------|
| **Causal discovery** | Identify which quality dimensions causally influence others, across sessions | Human reads Ρ — inter-agent conflicts |
| **Drift prediction** | Predict future quality trajectories from historical volume dynamics, before threshold crossing | Human reads Δ — trend signal |
| **Cross-pipeline conflict detection** | Identify conflicts spanning multiple sessions or pipelines | Human reads Ρ across time |
| **Policy generation** | Produce governance policies from historical patterns; pass to L2 for human review or direct injection | Human produces P_new at NCF |

The key distinction: L2 human orchestration requires the human to actively read {Ω, Δ, Ρ, Ξ} and inject policy. M(V) performs the equivalent inference automatically — without the human as the working-memory substrate, but accountable to the human at L2 for high-stakes decisions.

## 3. The L4 Efficiency Hypothesis

### 3.1 Formal Statement

> **L4 Efficiency Hypothesis (§6.2 CAL pre-paper):** there exists an inference architecture such that the cost of M(V) scales with κ(V) — the structural complexity of V (e.g., its effective rank, the entropy of its attractor distribution, the size of its causal graph) — where κ(V) grows **significantly more slowly than O(n²)** in n (the raw artifact count of the underlying L0 space).

### 3.2 What This Claims and What It Does Not

This is **not** a claim that meta-inference is exponentially cheaper than attention in all cases. It is a claim that:

> if semantic abstraction is semantically conservative (i.e., SID(Lk→Lk+1) > θ at each boundary), then inference cost can be decoupled from the volume of the original artifact stream and coupled instead to the structural complexity of the compressed governance state.

The claim is conditional on the success of L3. If C fails to compress V while preserving causal structure — if SID(L2→L3) < θ — then M(V) is inferring over a corrupted representation and the efficiency claim is moot.

### 3.3 Required Conditions

| Condition | Description | Status |
|-----------|-------------|--------|
| **(a)** C defined + κ(V) concrete | L3 composition operator validated on synthetic corpus; effective rank κ(V) measured | Pending — **gate L3** |
| **(b)** O(n²) baseline measured empirically | Flat-context attention cost on real hardware (MI300X) for seqLen 512→4k | **Active — AMD-Instinct** |
| **(c)** Governance accuracy under both approaches | Accuracy of M(V) vs. flat-context LLM on governance decisions; L2 corpus provides the reference | Pending — gate (a) |

**Pre-registration required before testing the hypothesis.** Commit the exact statistical test and acceptance criteria before running any experiment that bears on conditions (a) or (c).

### 3.4 κ(V) as Structural Complexity

κ(V) is the key operationalization. Three candidate definitions, ordered by tractability:

1. **Effective rank of Tucker core G** — most tractable; directly from L3's Tucker decomposition. κ(V) = rank(G).
2. **Attractor entropy** — entropy of the distribution over attractor states in V; requires identifying attractors first.
3. **Causal graph size** — number of edges in the recovered causal graph; requires S3 causal conservation test to succeed.

Candidate 1 is the starting point: it is immediately available if L3's Tucker decomposition succeeds.

## 4. The Representational Convergence Conjecture (RCC)

### 4.1 Statement

> **Representational Convergence Conjecture (§6.4 CAL pre-paper):** the representation that is *governance-optimal* (minimal sufficient for human-supervisable causal observability — CAL's V at high SID) and the representation that is *compute-optimal* (minimal sufficient internal state for the generator to continue producing semantically coherent output without re-deriving full context at each step) converge to the same object — or to two objects related by a tractable, possibly learned, translation.

### 4.2 Motivation

Current transformer inference pays O(n²) cost for context of *any* semantic structure — a 100k-token context describing a simple state costs the same as one describing a complex one. The L4 Efficiency Hypothesis (§3) observes that inference cost should couple to κ(V), not n. The RCC pushes one step further: if V persists as a compressed semantic substrate, a generator *coupled to V* would not re-generate semantic density from scratch at each step — it would emit *deltas* against the persistent state and let an update operator fold them in.

Under the RCC, the substrate doing this folding is *the same substrate CAL maintains for human governance*. The same V that a human reads at L2 NCF would be the V that the generator updates and reads during inference.

### 4.3 Why This Is Kept Separate from L2

L2 controls *pipeline transformation* — what the agentic system produces — via natural-language re-orchestration. The RCC concerns *compute-side transformability*: whether the generator can be reorganized to exploit the same compressed state.

These are different planes. L2 is governance over pipeline outputs. The RCC is a hypothesis about generator internals. **The CHI 2027 paper (TCO-L2) makes no RCC claims.** The RCC is L4-level speculation.

### 4.4 The Hard Requirement

The conjecture's difficulty is concentrated here: CAL's φ is today a *non-differentiable, human-legible* compressor (static analysis + discrete LLM-judge). For the RCC to hold, there must exist a φ′ that is simultaneously:
- Legible to a human supervisor (as φ currently is)
- Couplable to the generator's gradient or latent dynamics

Whether such φ′ exists — and whether the human-optimal and compute-optimal compressions actually coincide rather than merely correlate — is open. The negation is entirely plausible.

### 4.5 Relation to AMD-Instinct

`probe_mfma_mapping.hip` in AMD-Instinct already characterized the lane↔output mapping of `v_mfma_f32_16x16x16f16` — the low-level register access that coupling a generator to V at inference time would require. This makes the RCC empirically approachable from the hardware side, once C exists at L3.

### 4.6 Falsifiability

The RCC predicts:
- **(a)** For a generator coupled to persistent semantic state V, inference cost decouples from raw context length and couples to κ(V) (the L4 Efficiency Hypothesis as special case).
- **(b)** A single learned translation suffices to map between governance state V and generator update state, with bounded reconstruction loss on both governance accuracy *and* generation quality.

Failure of (b) — where minimizing governance-SID and minimizing generation loss require provably distinct representations — refutes the strong form.

not a toy. Correctness is checked on small configs (CPU reference) covering MHA, GQA,
and causal before the large timing-only runs.

## 7. Open Research Questions

1. **Minimal V for M:** what is the minimal V that supports M for a given class of governance decisions?
2. **Training signal:** can M be trained end-to-end from labeled governance outcomes, or does it require explicit causal structure in V as supervision?
3. **Failure mode characterization:** how does governance accuracy degrade when C fails to preserve causal structure?
4. **Scale threshold:** at what scale (agents, sessions, quality dimensions) does M(V) outperform flat-context LLM inference on governance tasks?
5. **RCC φ′ existence:** does a compressor exist that is simultaneously human-legible and generator-couplable?

## 8. SID at L4

SID(L3→L4) measures how much decision-relevant information is preserved through M. The measurement challenge at L4 is the same as at L3 — no human reference signal — but compounded: M operates on V rather than on T, and the governance decisions it produces span longer time horizons.

Three candidate measurement approaches (§7.3 CAL pre-paper):

1. **Downstream outcome tracking:** measure whether M(V) governance decisions lead to measurable pipeline improvements over time. Requires longitudinal deployment.
2. **Synthetic benchmarks with known causal structure:** M(V) decisions on synthetic pipelines with ground-truth causal graphs. Directly reuses L3's S3 infrastructure.
3. **L2 human comparison on held-out cases:** compare M(V) decisions against expert human L2 decisions on novel scenarios not seen during training.

Method 2 is the starting point: it is immediately composable with L3's synthetic corpus and does not require deployment.

## 9. Relationship to Existing Work

- **Flash Attention** [Dao et al. 2022]: IO-aware implementation of O(n²) attention — the baseline Rol 1 measures. `fa_dme` extends with DME async on MI300X.
- **State Space Models (Mamba/S4)** [Gu & Dao 2023]: demonstrate sub-quadratic temporal inference with persistent compressed state — structural analog of M(V) operating on V.
- **Mixture of Experts (MoE)**: token routing as a form of sparse inference — κ(V) as effective rank is related to the number of active experts needed for governance decisions.
- **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)**: V as a structured, semantically compressed knowledge base for governance — M as a specialized retriever/reasoner over V.
- **Tucker/CP Decomposition** [Kolda & Bader 2009]: L3's primary C candidate; defines κ(V) for L4.
- **Causal Representation Learning** [Schölkopf et al. 2021]: M must perform causal discovery over V — the same framework that constrains C at L3.
